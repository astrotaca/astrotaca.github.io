5. Your right to cancel

Under the Cancellation Act (angrerettloven) you can cancel your purchase within 14 days, without giving a reason.

The deadline

  • The 14 days start the day after you receive the item. If one order arrives in several parcels, they start the day after you receive the last one.
  • Every day counts. If the last day is a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday, the deadline moves to the next working day.
  • You are in time if you send your notice before the deadline ends.

How to cancel

Tell me that you are cancelling, in any clear way: through the contact form, by email or by letter. You can use the cancellation form at the bottom of this page, but you don't have to. Keep a copy of your message, since you may need to show that you cancelled in time. I confirm by email that I have received your notice.

Sending the item back

Send the item back without undue delay, and no later than 14 days after you told me you are cancelling. Use the address in section 1 unless I give you another one. You pay for the return shipping. Pack the item so it is not damaged on the way, and keep the receipt from the carrier.

Your refund

  • I refund everything you paid me, including the original shipping, within 14 days of receiving your notice. If you chose a more expensive delivery option than the cheapest one I offered, I refund the cost of the cheapest option.
  • I can wait with the refund until I have the item back, or until you have shown me that you have sent it, whichever comes first.
  • The refund goes back to the payment method you used, at no cost to you.
  • Import VAT or customs paid to your own country are not paid to me, so I can't refund them. Your carrier or customs office can tell you whether you can reclaim them.

Trying the item

You don't have to keep the item unopened. You can unpack it and try it the way you could in a shop: check that it fits your dew shield, switch it on, and connect it to your computer and software. If you handle the item more than you need to find out what it is, its features and how it works, and its value goes down as a result, I can take the loss in value off your refund.

6. Late or missing delivery

If I don't deliver on time, and it isn't because of something you did, you can under the Consumer Purchases Act:

  • insist that I deliver, unless that is impossible or would cost me far more than it is worth to you;
  • cancel the purchase and get your money back, if the delay is significant, or if I don't deliver within a reasonable extra deadline you set;
  • claim compensation for losses the delay causes you, unless the delay was caused by something outside my control.

Tell me in writing, for example through the contact form.

7. Faults

If the item has a fault, the Consumer Purchases Act gives you these rights.

Deadlines for telling me

  • Tell me within a reasonable time after you notice the fault, or should have noticed it. You are always in time if you tell me within 2 months of noticing it.
  • You can report a fault for 2 years after you received the item. For items, or parts of items, that are meant to last much longer than 2 years in normal use, the limit is 5 years.

What you can ask for

  • Repair or replacement, whichever you choose, unless your choice is impossible or would cost me unreasonably much compared with the other. I cover the costs, including shipping both ways, and do it within a reasonable time. As a rule, I get no more than two attempts to fix the same fault.
  • A price reduction, if the item is not repaired or replaced.
  • Cancelling the purchase and getting your money back, if the item is not repaired or replaced and the fault is not minor.
  • Compensation for losses the fault causes you.

To report a fault, use the contact form. Describe what happens, include your receipt number and, if you can, a photo or short video. I'm happy to help you troubleshoot first, but that never shortens your deadlines.

Any warranty from me or from a manufacturer comes on top of your rights under sections 6 and 7. It never limits your right to complain about faults or late delivery.

10. Complaints and disputes

If something goes wrong, contact me first, and we'll try to solve it together.

If we can't agree, you can ask the Norwegian Consumer Authority, Forbrukertilsynet, to mediate. If mediation doesn't solve it, the case can go to the Consumer Disputes Commission, Forbrukerklageutvalget, which decides disputes much like a court. Its decisions are binding, but either of us can take the case to the courts.

If you live in another EU or EEA country, Forbruker Europa, the European Consumer Centre in Norway, can help with cross-border disputes.
