AstroTaca

Motorized Flat Panel

Make flats part of your imaging routine.

A motorized cover and dimmable flat-field light that stays on your telescope. Let NINA handle your flats automatically, or take control from the AstroTaca app and the button on the panel.

Flats while you sleep

With your NINA sequence set up, the cover, light and flat exposures can run unattended. No trip back to the telescope to hold a light over the scope.

4,096 levels of brightness

Fine-tune your flat-field light with 12-bit brightness control. Let NINA's Flat Wizard adjust the brightness automatically, or choose the level yourself in the AstroTaca app.

An opening that suits your rig

Set a shorter opening when your setup needs it. The saved setting is used by NINA, the app and the button, with your full opening kept separately.

At the end of an imaging session

Close. Illuminate. Capture.

Keep your camera and imaging train in place. A configured NINA sequence can handle the cover, light and flat exposures together, so calibration becomes part of the night rather than another task at the telescope.

The light only turns on with the cover closed. Opening switches it off.

  1. 01

    Close the cover

    Ask NINA or the AstroTaca app to close the panel, then wait for it to settle.

  2. 02

    Capture your flats

    Use Flat Wizard for automatic brightness, or your chosen flat settings in a NINA sequence.

  3. 03

    Finish your sequence

    Switch the light off when done. Open the cover when you are ready to image again.

At your desk or beside the telescope

Your choice of control

The AstroTaca Windows app gives you cover controls, a brightness slider and guided setup. It can stay connected alongside NINA, so closing the app does not disconnect your imaging software.

For hands-on sessions, use the panel's button to open, close, stop and cycle three saved brightness presets. Set those levels once in the app, then use them without a computer connected.

Built from my own imaging routine

From an improvised light to a dedicated panel

Taking flats used to mean a T-shirt over the scope or a tablet showing a white screen. The rest of my setup was already automated, so I started building a panel that could fit into the same routine.

The project now has working prototype controls in NINA and the Windows app, saved button brightness and adjustable opening settings. The complete panel is still being developed and tested, including mounting, illumination and use on the telescope. Updated product images will follow as the design progresses.
